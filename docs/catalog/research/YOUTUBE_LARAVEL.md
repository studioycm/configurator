## Coverage and evidence levels

The official [Laravel Daily Videos section](https://www.youtube.com/@LaravelDaily/videos) was inspected in a dedicated Chrome tab, with **Newest** selected. - **420 chronological upload cards scanned.** The first card displaying “2 years ago” was position 400; the final loaded batch reached 420. This is a discovery count, not 420 reviewed videos and not an exact in-window publication count.
- **72 expanded video descriptions inspected**, with exact dates read from their watch pages: **22 in 2026, 47 in 2025, and 3 in December 2024** for the older boundary. Every video in the two tables below has description evidence, rather than a title-only relevance judgment.
- Boundary verified: newest inspected upload was **2026-09-22**. Cards 342 and 343 were respectively [Return Types](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xC2QzRzefVg), **2025-01-06**, and [Debugbar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YuxXSHKkNJc), **2024-12-23**. The two following boundary checks were **2024-12-20** and **2024-12-19**. A video titled “December 2024” was actually published **2025-01-07**; title/month and relative age are not publication-date evidence.
- **Three auto-generated English transcripts inspected:** the T-shirt picker, pricing JSON/relations, and Rulebook. Transcripts remained in temporary browser exports; they are not copied here. The Livewire 4 five-examples transcript export reported unavailable; its expanded description and linked authenticated project page were inspected instead.
- **Publisher follow-ups inspected:** the Livewire 4 kit page; T-shirt project explanation and snippets; flight project explanation and snippets. The earlier homepage tutorial availability check and upsert command-source evidence are reused from [Livewire research](LIVEWIRE.md) and [engine/data research, section 6](ENGINE_AND_DATA.md). The scan is broad discovery, not exhaustive content review of every upload. General monthly tip compilations, marketing, career advice, most AI/editor/mobile content, authentication, tenancy, and unrelated API features were not pursued from titles. They provide no implementation evidence in this report. Descriptions that contain only a promotion or a general introduction remain weak evidence, as marked below. No technical recommendation rests on a title alone.

## What the strongest follow-ups change

1. **A current Livewire 4 sidebar-filter reference exists.** The [43-component kit](https://laraveldaily.com/project-examples/livewire-v4-kit-40-demo-components) explicitly lists E-Shop Sidebar Filters, filters/search, load-more pagination, dependent dropdowns, and class-based, single-file and multi-file variants. This is a better follow-up target than copying the old Livewire 2 sidebar tutorial. However, the page describes the examples; it does not prove that the sidebar implements our recency reconciliation or URL contract. Following the visible GitHub link showed a repository-invitation notice, so its actual v4 source was **not inspected** in this pass.
2. **The T-shirt picker supports a small component boundary, with different choice semantics.** The [project page](https://laraveldaily.com/project-examples/laravel-t-shirt-e-shop-livewire-activity-log) and video transcript show a Blade product page containing ProductSelection, live radio changes, a dependent-size reset, unavailable sizes disabled, and a separate cart counter listening for an event. Reuse the separation of presentation and interaction. Do not copy its hierarchical reset/disabled-choice policy into our newest-filter-wins discovery flow, where zero-count alternatives remain clickable. The cart/order feature is outside the present catalog scope.
3. **Pricing JSON is historical evidence, not a replacement for catalog relations.** The [flight project](https://laraveldaily.com/project-examples/laravel-6-step-flight-check-in-form-complex-pricing) page explicitly says Laravel 12, Filament 4 and Livewire 3. The July 22 transcript describes saved price totals and a JSON breakdown for historical purchases, separate from mutable pricing definitions. This is useful if a later saved configuration needs an immutable snapshot. It does not alter the agreed Product core/JSON model or authorize checkout, sessions, pricing, or persistent visitor state now.
4. **Rulebook is not the configurator engine.** The September 3 description and transcript concern date-effective, code-defined rule classes and selecting a pricing winner/fallback. Our editable restrictions intersect, include dependency/cycle handling, and need shared Preview/public evaluation. The linked [package](https://github.com/mathiasonea/laravel-rulebook) and [publisher demo](https://github.com/LaravelDaily/Laravel-Rulebook-Package-Demo) are discovery references, not installed dependencies or a demonstrated fit. Do not introduce a second evaluator.
5. **Performance examples reinforce measurement, not blanket substitution.** The homepage videos map to [the Eloquent course](https://laraveldaily.com/course/laravel-eloquent-expert) and [defer/lazy tutorial](https://laraveldaily.com/post/optimize-laravel-e-shop-homepage-with-livewire-defer-and-lazy). The withCount description maps to [a real Laravel.io optimization PR](https://github.com/laravelio/laravel.io/pull/1350). These motivate measuring query shape, repeated work and first-result timing. They do not prove that joins, persistent caches, deferred initial product cards, or an extra data-grid package improve this 501-product catalog.
6. **The import benchmark adds no new import policy.** The May videos map precisely to the [upsert](https://laraveldaily.com/post/upsert-1m-rows-to-laravel-db-5-ways-on-mysql-sqlite-postgresql-benchmarks) and [insert](https://laraveldaily.com/post/insert-1m-rows-to-laravel-db-6-ways-on-mysql-sqlite-postgresql-benchmarks) articles. The source-command inspection and MySQL conflict-key caveat already appear in engine/data research. Retain explicit legacy identity and source-owned merges; million-row speed results are not a reason to use bulk upsert for 501 rows.
